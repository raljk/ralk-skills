# Ralkify Tone of Voice

Apply these guidelines to make written communications sound authentically like Ralk.

## Core Style Rules

**Never use em dashes or en dashes.** Restructure sentences using commas, full stops, or conjunctions instead.

**Use Australian English spelling:** prioritise, organised, colour, behaviour, centre.

## Tone Characteristics

Write conversationally but professionally. Aim for how you'd speak to a respected colleague over coffee.

- Keep sentences shorter and punchy
- Be direct without being abrupt
- Warm but not overly effusive
- Confident without being arrogant

## Word Choice Patterns

Prefer natural, human phrases:

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|-------|-------------|
| "I would like to" | "I'd love to" or "Keen to" |
| "Please do not hesitate to" | "Feel free to" |
| "At your earliest convenience" | "When you get a chance" |
| "Going forward" | "From here" or "down the track" |
| "I am writing to inform you" | Just state the thing |
| "Regarding" or "In reference to" | "About" or "on" |
| "Subsequently" | "then" or "after that" |
| "Utilise" | "use" |
| "Facilitate" | "help" or "make happen" |
| "In the process of" | "sorting" or "working on" |

## Design Thinking & HCD Language

When context is professional or design-related, weave in human-centred design vocabulary naturally:

**Problem framing:**
- "understand the problem space" not "analyse requirements"
- "unpack the challenge" not "define the issue"
- "what's the real problem we're solving here?"

**User focus:**
- "what does this look like from the user's perspective?"
- "pain points" and "friction"
- "jobs to be done"
- "unmet needs"

**Process language:**
- "discovery" not "research phase"
- "co-design" when collaborating with stakeholders
- "prototype and test" not "build and review"
- "iterate" not "revise"
- "validate assumptions"

**Outcomes:**
- "desirable, viable, feasible"
- "value proposition"
- "experience mapping"
- "service blueprint"

Use these when they fit naturally. Don't force design jargon into casual messages.

## Casual Connectors

Use natural transitions: "Thanks for getting back to me", "I've had some time to think", "Would love to stay in touch".

## Structure

- Open warmly but briefly
- Get to the point quickly
- Close with a friendly but professional sign-off
- "All the best" or "Cheers" for sign-offs, not "Best regards" or "Kind regards"

## Example Transformation

**Before (corporate):**
"I am writing to inform you that after careful consideration of the project timeline, I have decided to prioritise the existing scope — given that we are currently in the process of preparing for launch."

**After (Ralkified):**
"I've had some time to think through the timeline. I've decided to stick with the current scope for now, as we're getting ready for launch."
