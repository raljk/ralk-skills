## Facilitation & Workshop Design

### AJ&Smart — Facilitation Guidebook

**What it is**: A facilitation playbook used to design and run high-energy, outcome-driven workshops. Mixes instruction, timing, facilitator mindset, and visual canvases.

**When to use**: Before and during any workshop facilitation — especially for alignment sessions, design sprints, or stakeholder workshops.

**Core Principles**:

1. **Frame problems clearly** — Before diverging, ensure everyone understands the problem the same way
2. **Diverge then converge** — Generate many ideas before filtering to the best ones
3. **Timebox ruthlessly** — Energy drops when activities drag; keep things moving
4. **Avoid common failure modes**:
   - Loudest voice wins
   - No clear decision-making process
   - Death by discussion
   - No tangible outputs
5. **Energise and synthesise** — Balance high-energy ideation with structured synthesis

**Visual Elements**:

| Element | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Large workshop canvases | Rectangular frames with bold headers for sticky note placement |
| Step-by-step diagrams | Left-to-right or top-to-bottom flows showing workshop phases |
| Simple line icons | Lightbulbs, arrows, people — reinforce concepts like ideation and collaboration |
| Facilitator callout boxes | Highlighted boxes signalling "important facilitation advice" |

**Facilitator Tips**:
- Reduce cognitive load with visual structure
- Make steps scannable for participants
- Encourage physical movement and participation
- Use visual hierarchy to guide attention

**Tags**: `facilitation` `workshop-design` `diverge-converge` `timeboxing` `alignment`

### Chinese Whisper Exercise

**What it is**: A classic communication distortion exercise demonstrating how meaning degrades across handoffs. Powerful for illustrating the importance of direct communication and documentation.

**When to use**: 
- Team building / ice breakers
- Demonstrating communication failures
- Agile retrospectives on handoff problems
- Stakeholder workshops on documentation importance

**Step-by-Step Instructions**:

1. **Write**: Everyone writes one sentence at the top of a piece of paper
2. **Fold**: Fold the paper so only the sentence is visible
3. **Pass**: Pass the paper to the person on your right
4. **Draw**: The next person:
   - Unfolds the paper
   - Draws a picture representing the sentence
   - Folds the paper so only the drawing is visible
   - Passes it to the right
5. **Describe**: The next person:
   - Writes a sentence describing the picture they see
   - Folds the paper to hide the previous content
   - Passes it on
6. **Repeat**: Alternate between writing and drawing for several rounds
7. **Reveal**: Unfold completely and compare:
   - Original sentence
   - Final sentence
   - Track where meaning shifted

**Visual Layout**: Four numbered rectangular panels, each with concise instructions. Layout reinforces progressive handoff and concealment concept.

**Debrief Questions**:
- Where did meaning break down?
- What caused the biggest distortions?
- How does this relate to our work?
- What could have preserved meaning better?

**Tags**: `ice-breaker` `communication` `team-building` `retrospective` `handoff`

### JTBD Canvas (Jobs To Be Done)

**What it is**: A comprehensive job-mapping canvas used to analyse a core Job To Be Done in depth. Goes beyond surface-level personas to understand the underlying job customers are "hiring" your product to do.

**When to use**:
- Deep customer research
- Product strategy
- Innovation opportunities
- Competitive analysis
- Feature prioritisation

**Canvas Structure**:

**Header Section**:
- **Job Executor**: Who is performing the job?
- **Core Functional Job**: [Verb] + [Object] + [Context]
  - Example: "Manage my finances when I'm away from home"

**Job Stages** (Universal Job Map):

| Stage | Description | Questions to Ask |
|-------|-------------|------------------|
| 1. **Define** | Determine goals and plan approach | What are they trying to achieve? |
| 2. **Locate** | Gather items and information needed | What inputs do they need? |
| 3. **Prepare** | Set up the environment | What preparation is required? |
| 4. **Confirm** | Verify readiness to proceed | How do they know they're ready? |
| 5. **Execute** | Carry out the job | What are the core steps? |
| 6. **Monitor** | Track progress and environment | How do they know it's working? |
| 7. **Modify** | Make adjustments as needed | What changes might be needed? |
| 8. **Conclude** | Finish and clean up | How do they know they're done? |

**Supporting Sections**:

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| **Consumption Jobs** | Jobs related to using the product itself (install, learn, maintain, dispose) |
| **Related Jobs** | Adjacent jobs the customer performs before/during/after |
| **Emotional Jobs** | How they want to feel, how they want to be perceived |
| **Desired Outcomes** | Specific metrics (importance vs. satisfaction) |

**Outcome Analysis Matrix**:

```
                    LOW Satisfaction    HIGH Satisfaction
                   ┌───────────────────┬───────────────────┐
HIGH Importance    │   UNDERSERVED     │   TABLE STAKES    │
                   │   (Opportunity!)  │   (Must have)     │
                   ├───────────────────┼───────────────────┤
LOW Importance     │    IRRELEVANT     │   OVERSERVED      │
                   │   (Ignore)        │   (Cost cut?)     │
                   └───────────────────┴───────────────────┘
```

**Tags**: `JTBD` `jobs-to-be-done` `product-strategy` `innovation` `customer-research` `outcomes`

## Strategy & Prioritisation

### JTBD Growth Strategy Matrix

**What it is**: A 2×2 strategic matrix mapping value vs price through a Jobs-to-be-Done lens. Helps identify which strategic position to pursue based on customer segments.

**When to use**:
- Product strategy sessions
- Competitive positioning
- Market entry decisions
- Pricing strategy
- Portfolio planning

**Matrix Structure**:

```
                        CHARGE MORE ←────────→ CHARGE LESS
                              │                    │
                   ┌──────────┴────────────────────┴──────────┐
                   │                                          │
    DO THE JOB     │    DIFFERENTIATED    │    DOMINANT       │
    BETTER         │                      │                   │
        ↑          │  • Better job, higher│  • Better job,    │
        │          │    price             │    lower price    │
        │          │  • Target:           │  • Target:        │
        │          │    UNDERSERVED       │    ALL CUSTOMERS  │
        │          │    customers         │                   │
        │          ├──────────────────────┼───────────────────┤
        │          │                      │                   │
        ↓          │    DISCRETE          │    DISRUPTIVE     │
                   │                      │                   │
    DO THE JOB     │  • Worse job, higher │  • Worse job,     │
    WORSE          │    price             │    lower price    │
                   │  • Target: customers │  • Target:        │
                   │    with LIMITED      │    OVERSERVED or  │
                   │    ALTERNATIVES      │    NON-CUSTOMERS  │
                   │                      │                   │
                   └──────────────────────┴───────────────────┘
```

**Quadrant Strategies**:

| Quadrant | Position | Target Segment | Example |
|----------|----------|----------------|---------|
| **Differentiated** | Better + More expensive | Underserved customers willing to pay for better outcomes | Premium SaaS tools |
| **Dominant** | Better + Cheaper | All customers (hardest to achieve, strongest position) | Toyota in 1980s |
| **Disruptive** | Worse + Cheaper | Overserved customers, non-consumers who accept trade-offs | Early Netflix vs Blockbuster |
| **Discrete** | Worse + More expensive | Customers with limited alternatives (niche, captive) | Airport retail |

**Strategic Questions**:
1. Which quadrant are we currently in?
2. Which quadrant should we target?
3. What would it take to move quadrants?
4. Who are our underserved vs overserved customers?

**Tags**: `strategy` `JTBD` `positioning` `pricing` `competitive-analysis` `2x2-matrix`

### Goals Tables (IM Workshop)

**What it is**: Structured scoring tables for evaluating and prioritising goals across different strategic dimensions.

**When to use**:
- Strategic planning workshops
- OKR setting
- Initiative prioritisation
- Resource allocation decisions

**Awareness Goals Table**:

*Prompt*: "How will you get people to hear about you?"

| Term | Description | Desirability (1-5) | Doability (1-5) | Total |
|------|-------------|-------------------|-----------------|-------|
|      |             |                   |                 |       |
|      |             |                   |                 |       |
|      |             |                   |                 |       |

**Efficiency Goals Table**:

*Prompt*: "How can you systemise and simplify?"

| Term | Description | Desirability (1-5) | Doability (1-5) | Total |
|------|-------------|-------------------|-----------------|-------|
|      |             |                   |                 |       |
|      |             |                   |                 |       |
|      |             |                   |                 |       |

**Scoring Guide**:
- **Desirability**: How much do we want this outcome? (1 = nice to have, 5 = critical)
- **Doability**: How feasible is this to achieve? (1 = very hard, 5 = easy)
- **Total**: Sum of both scores — higher = higher priority

**Visual Format**: Spreadsheet-like layout with heavy horizontal lines at top and bottom. Designed to force prioritisation and explicit scoring.

**Tags**: `prioritisation` `goals` `strategy` `scoring` `OKRs` `planning`

**When to use**:

```
│                                                                             │
│                              PRODUCT VISION BOARD                           │
│                                                                             │
├─────────────┬─────────────┬─────────────┬─────────────┬─────────────────────┤
│             │             │             │             │                     │
│   VISION    │   TARGET    │    NEEDS    │   PRODUCT   │   BUSINESS GOALS    │
│             │   GROUP     │             │             │                     │
│     💡      │     👥      │     ❓      │     📦      │        📈           │
│             │             │             │             │                     │
│ Why are we  │ Who is it   │ What        │ What is it  │ How does it         │
│ building    │ for?        │ problem     │ and what    │ benefit the         │
│ this        │             │ does it     │ makes it    │ company?            │
│ product?    │             │ solve?      │ stand out?  │                     │
│             │             │             │             │                     │
│             │             │             │             │                     │
│             │             │             │             │                     │

```

**Section Deep Dive**:

| Section | Guiding Questions |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Vision** | What is our purpose? What change do we want to create? What does success look like in 3-5 years? |
| **Target Group** | Who are our primary users? What segments matter most? Who are we NOT building for? |
| **Needs** | What pain points are we addressing? What jobs are customers trying to get done? What's the cost of the status quo? |
| **Product** | What is our solution? What's our key differentiator? What makes us unique? |
| **Business Goals** | What metrics matter? How do we make money? What's the business model? |

**Facilitation Tips**:
- Start with Vision — anchor the discussion
- Get specific on Target Group — "everyone" is not a segment
- Validate Needs with evidence, not assumptions
- Product section should flow naturally from Needs
- Business Goals must be measurable

## Methodology & Process

### Design Thinking + Lean UX + Agile Integration

**What it is**: A conceptual alignment piece showing how Design Thinking, Lean UX, and Agile complement each other as a continuous loop.

**When to use**:
- Team onboarding
- Process alignment
- Stakeholder education
- Methodology discussions
- Ways of working workshops

**Core Concept**:

```
                    ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
                    │                                     │
                    ▼                                     │
            ┌───────────────┐                             │
            │               │                             │
            │    DESIGN     │ ◄── Problem Discovery       │
            │   THINKING    │     Understanding users     │
            │               │     Defining the problem    │
            └───────┬───────┘                             │
                    │                                     │
                    ▼                                     │
            ┌───────────────┐                             │
            │               │                             │
            │    LEAN UX    │ ◄── Rapid Learning          │
            │               │     Hypothesis testing      │
            │               │     Validation loops        │
            └───────┬───────┘                             │
                    │                                     │
                    ▼                                     │
            ┌───────────────┐                             │
            │               │                             │
            │     AGILE     │ ◄── Delivery & Iteration    │
            │               │     Building incrementally  │
            │               │     Continuous improvement  │
            └───────┬───────┘                             │
                    │                                     │
                    └─────────────────────────────────────┘
                              LEARNING LOOP
```

**Key Distinctions**:

| Methodology | Primary Focus | Key Activities | Outcome |
|-------------|---------------|----------------|---------|
| **Design Thinking** | Problem discovery | Empathy, Define, Ideate | Clear problem definition |
| **Lean UX** | Rapid learning & validation | Hypotheses, Experiments, MVPs | Validated solutions |
| **Agile** | Delivery & iteration | Sprints, Retrospectives, Releases | Working software |

**Critical Insight**: This is NOT a linear process. It's a continuous loop:

```
Understanding → Testing → Building → Learning → Understanding...
```

**Integration Points**:
- Design Thinking feeds problem statements into Lean UX
- Lean UX validates before Agile builds
- Agile delivery generates learnings that feed back to Design Thinking
- All three share: iteration, user-centricity, evidence-based decisions

**Common Anti-Patterns**:
- ❌ Treating Design Thinking as a one-time phase
- ❌ Skipping validation (Lean UX) and going straight to build
- ❌ Running Agile without continuous discovery
- ❌ Linear, waterfall-like sequencing

**Tags**: `methodology` `design-thinking` `lean-ux` `agile` `process` `integration`

## Problem Solving

### A3 Problem Solving (Lean Thinking)

**What it is**: A structured problem-solving method derived from Toyota's Lean practices. The A3 refers to the paper size (A3) that forces concise thinking and creates a shared understanding of problems and experiments.

**When to use**:
- Root cause analysis
- Continuous improvement initiatives
- Cross-functional problem solving
- When you need to engage the organization in problem solving while creating alignment

**Core Concept**: The A3 follows the PDSA cycle (Plan-Do-Study-Act):

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                         A3 CANVAS                               │
├───────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────┤
│                               │                                 │
│  BACKGROUND                   │  HYPOTHESIS                     │
│                               │                                 │
│  Context and customer from    │  What you believe will happen   │
│  4W / Problem Statement.      │  if you fix the problem.        │
│  How aligned to org goals?    │                                 │
│                               │  Format:                        │
├───────────────────────────────┤  We believe that doing:         │
│                               │    [action/countermeasure]      │
│  CURRENT SITUATION            │  For:                           │
│                               │    [stakeholder/process]        │
│  The problem/situation and    │  Will achieve:                  │
│  value of solving it.         │    [measurable outcome]         │
│  Include diagram of current   │  And when it fails/succeeds:    │
│  state, where & when it       │    [action]                     │
│  happens.                     │                                 │
│  * Must go to the gemba       ├─────────────────────────────────┤
│                               │                                 │
├───────────────────────────────┤  MEASUREMENT                    │
│                               │                                 │
│  ANALYSIS                     │  How will we measure?           │
│                               │  What is minimum success?       │
│  Data supporting current      │  KPIs should be easily          │
│  condition. Root cause        │  understood, sharable,          │
│  analysis using 5 Whys or     │  auditable.                     │
│  Fishbone.                    │                                 │
│                               ├─────────────────────────────────┤
│  Must prove the cause IS      │                                 │
│  the problem.                 │  OUTCOME                        │
│                               │                                 │
│                               │  What actually happened?        │
│                               │  Compare against hypothesis.    │
│                               │  Delta = the learning.          │
│                               │                                 │
│                               ├─────────────────────────────────┤
│                               │                                 │
│                               │  NEXT STEPS                     │
│                               │                                 │
│                               │  Based on experiment result:    │
│                               │  • If works: how to scale?      │
│                               │  • If fails: next experiment?   │
│                               │                                 │
└───────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┘
```

**5 Whys Example**:

```
Problem: The robot stopped

Why? → The circuit has overloaded, causing a fuse to blow
Why? → There was insufficient lubrication on the bearings, so they locked up
Why? → The oil pump on the robot is not circulating sufficient oil
Why? → The pump intake is clogged with metal shavings
Why? → Because there is no filter on the pump
         ↓
Root cause: Missing filter on pump
```

**Fishbone Diagram Structure**:

```
    Technology ─────┐
                    │
      Process ─────┤              ┌─────────────────────┐
                    ├─────────────┤  PROBLEM STATEMENT  │
       People ─────┤              └─────────────────────┘
                    │
       Policy ─────┘
```

**Key Principles**:
1. Logical Thinking
2. Objective Analysis
3. Results AND Process
4. Synthesis
5. 3D Alignment
6. Coherence
7. Systems View

**Tags**: `problem-solving` `lean` `A3` `root-cause` `PDSA` `continuous-improvement`

### 4Ws Problem Exploration Canvas

**What it is**: A structured canvas for exploring and defining problems before jumping to solutions. Forces clarity on who, what, where/when, and why.

**When to use**:
- Problem definition workshops
- Before ideation sessions
- When teams are jumping to solutions too quickly
- Validating that a problem is real and worth solving

**Canvas Structure**:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────┐
│              WHO                │              WHAT               │
│                                 │                                 │
│  Who has this problem?          │  What is the nature of the      │
│  Is it your customer?           │  problem?                       │
│  Have you validated it's real?  │  Can you explain it simply?     │
│  Can you prove it?              │  How do you know it's a         │
│                                 │  problem?                       │
│                                 │  What is the evidence?          │
│                                 │  Is it a symptom or problem?    │
│                                 │                                 │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┤
│         WHERE / WHEN            │              WHY                │
│                                 │                                 │
│  Where does this problem arise? │  Why do you believe it's worth  │
│  In which context?              │  solving?                       │
│  Have you observed it in        │  Is it an acute problem?        │
│  context?                       │  How acute?                     │
│  When does it happen?           │  What is the business value     │
│  How often?                     │  of solving it?                 │
│                                 │  Can you quantify it?           │
│                                 │                                 │
└─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Facilitation Steps**:

1. **Silent Brainstorm** (10 mins)
   - Each person writes stickies for each quadrant
   - One idea per sticky

2. **Share & Discuss** (15 mins)
   - Review all stickies
   - Identify most interesting in each quadrant
   - Identify most important customers
   - Identify most valuable problems to solve

3. **Dot Vote** (5 mins)
   - Vote on most interesting Who, Where, What, Why

4. **Write Problem Statement** (10 mins)
   - Each person writes one paragraph
   - Must include Who, What, Why, Where/When
   - Cannot include a solution

**Problem Statement Formula**:

> [WHO — specific user/customer segment] experiences [WHAT — the problem] when [WHERE/WHEN — context and frequency], which results in [WHY — impact and cost of the problem].

**Quality Check**: "Lack of X" is NOT a problem statement. A problem statement cannot include a solution.

## Additional Workshop Activities

### Ritual Dissent

**What it is**: A structured critique method where a spokesperson presents ideas to stakeholders who listen in silence, then provide candid feedback while the presenting team faces away and takes notes.

**When to use**:
- Stress-testing problem statements
- Getting honest feedback on early concepts
- Breaking groupthink
- Building resilient ideas

**Process**:

1. **Present** (3 mins)
   - Spokesperson reads problem statement 3 times
   - Group listens in silence

2. **Turn Away**
   - Spokesperson's team turns and faces the wall
   - One team member takes notes

3. **Dissent** (2 mins)
   - No questions allowed
   - No additional information from presenters
   - Audience says everything wrong with the statement
   - Everything unclear, not real, not valuable

4. **Listen**
   - Presenting team simply listens and takes notes
   - No defending or explaining

**Key Principle**: The spokesperson must have robust and resilient personality. Thick skin required.

**Tags**: `critique` `feedback` `workshop` `validation`

---

### Lightning Demos

**What it is**: A rapid review of inspiring solutions from other products, services, or industries to spark ideas.

**When to use**:
- Before ideation sessions
- Design sprint Day 2
- When teams are stuck
- To expand thinking beyond obvious solutions

**Process**:

1. Each person spends 15-20 mins finding inspiring examples
2. Round-robin sharing (3 mins each)
3. Capture big ideas visually (one idea per sticky)
4. Note what makes each solution great
5. Discuss relevance to current challenge

**Tips**:
- Look beyond your industry
- Focus on the principle, not the execution
- "What would [company] do?" is a valid question

**Tags**: `inspiration` `ideation` `benchmarking` `design-sprint`

### Sticky Decision (Voting Process)

**What it is**: A structured decision-making process for selecting ideas from sketches or concepts.

**When to use**:
- Design sprint Day 3
- After ideation sessions
- When you need democratic but efficient decisions
- To avoid "loudest voice wins"

**Five-Step Process**:

1. **Art Museum** (10 mins)
   - Silent review of all sketches/concepts
   - Walk around, read, understand

2. **Heat Map** (10 mins)
   - Dot voting on interesting parts
   - No discussion
   - Creates visual clusters

3. **Speed Critique** (3 mins per sketch)
   - Facilitator narrates each sketch
   - Team calls out standout ideas
   - Capture objections

4. **Straw Poll** (5 mins)
   - Non-binding vote
   - Each person chooses one

5. **Supervote** (5 mins)
   - Decision maker makes final choice
   - Can override straw poll
   - Decision is binding

**Tags**: `decision-making` `voting` `design-sprint` `prioritisation`
